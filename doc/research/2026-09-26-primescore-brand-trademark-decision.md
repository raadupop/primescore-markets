# PrimeScore: portfolio naming, brand architecture and preliminary trademark clearance

Investigation date: 2026-09-26. Prepared as decision support for the founder (Radu Pop). This is not a legal opinion, a clearance letter, a guarantee of registration or privileged advice. Every register status below is as found on 2026-09-26; time-sensitive items are marked. Companion files: [evidence log](2026-09-26-primescore-evidence-log.md) and [query log](2026-09-26-primescore-query-log.md).

Method: nine research modules ran as parallel agents on 2026-09-26 (EU/Romania registers; WIPO; USPTO and UKIPO; IP India, Sri Lanka and company records; PrimeScore.in; other users; .ai domain; EU/Romanian law; UK/US/India law), plus four naming directions and two judges. Finalist screening and the adversarial review were done in the main session after the second agent run was cut off by a usage limit; the coverage statement in the appendix says exactly which checks are lighter. On 2026-09-27 the founder rejected Loupe as not premium enough, and a premium-word round of 31 candidates was screened the same way; Aplomb replaced Loupe.

## Decision brief

**Finance product: Aplomb.** Descriptor "A clear, evidence-based picture of your money across every bank"; endorsed "Aplomb, by PrimeScore" in the footer only. Naming choice on merit after Loupe was rejected as not premium. Adoption status: pause pending one named check, an APLOMB search in the EUIPO, OSIM and UKIPO registers (classes 9, 36, 42), because software-services companies named Aplomb exist and no EU register could be searched. If that check fails, adopt Composure.

**PrimeScore: keep, with restrictions.** It remains the umbrella for the professional products. It never leads the consumer finance product or presents a "score" as an output, and is filed only after a professional similarity search. On brand grounds alone both naming judges preferred replacement: the string reads as a credit-score brand and "Score" misdescribes four of five products. The evidence points the other way: no PRIMESCORE mark exists in EUIPO, OSIM, WIPO or UKIPO data; both US applications are dead; the Indian namesake holds only an objected device-mark application and trades only in India; and every screened replacement (Upshot, Readout, Evident, Groundtruth) has identical-word marks or businesses in adjacent classes and no available domain.

**Architecture:** one umbrella with new labels for the professional family, one independently named consumer product, and Monitor demoted to a capability.

### Finance shortlist, ranked

1. **Aplomb.** Strength: a real word in English and Romanian meaning confident self-possession; premium and distinctive; no finance company or finance app of that name found in the US, UK or Romanian stores; it promises poise, not an action. Weakness: software-development companies use the word; every short aplomb domain is taken; English drops the final b, Romanian sounds it.
2. **Composure.** Strength: calm control over money; no finance use found. Weakness: three syllables, a legal-software startup uses it, and it says less than Aplomb. Aplomb wins: shorter, more distinctive, livelier.
3. **Quotient.** Strength: premium, analytical, an IQ echo; no finance app found. Weakness: Quotient Technology is a large US software mark.
4. **SpendSignal.** Strength: transparent, family-coherent. Weakness: seven syllables and a B2B marketing SaaS uses the string. Functional fallback only.

Twenty-eight other premium words were screened and dropped; the list is in Appendix G.

### Tradeoff and verified position

The solo-founder tradeoff is one identity to maintain against a permanent "not a credit score, no brokerage" line. A rename costs hours today and more each month, but the replacements are no cleaner. Verified: no identical earlier mark in the launch territory; a crowded field of SCORE and PRIME composites that a similarity opinion must assess; absolute-grounds risk for anything sold as a score; an India-only namesake with an objected application; US "Prime Score" credit-repair shops, relevant only to a US consumer launch; primescore.ai registered by the founder on 2026-09-21, so its dispute posture rests on documented preparations. Unverified: direct searches of every official register; designation status of international SCORE marks; APLOMB marks in the EU and UK; whether Wesper Tech SRL should be the applicant.

### Actions

Today: proceed with restrictions. Adopt the labels in code and copy, keep Aplomb unpublished, preserve dated evidence (domain receipt, repository history), request a Wayback capture of primescore.ai, and record when you first learned of primescore.in.

1. Commission a professional EU and Romanian similarity search and absolute-grounds opinion for PRIMESCORE in classes 9, 35, 36 and 42, and decide the filing entity and route before Markets has external users.
2. Run the APLOMB decisive check; if blocked, switch to Composure and rescreen.
3. If the PRIMESCORE opinion is negative, rename before launch; Upshot is the screened fallback, subject to its own EU search.

Tests applied, in brief. Registration in the EU and Romania is blocked only by earlier rights listed in EUTMR Art. 8(2) (EU, national and international marks and applications, and Paris 6bis well-known marks), by non-registered signs used in the course of trade of more than mere local significance (Art. 8(4); Romanian Art. 6(3)(c)), by marks with reputation (Art. 8(5)), and, on the Romanian national route, by a mark protected abroad where the applicant acted in bad faith (Art. 6(3)(f)). An Indian company registration and an abandoned US application are in none of those categories. Likelihood of confusion is assessed globally (SABEL §22–23) with interdependence between sign and service similarity (Canon §17) and by the Canon factors for services (nature, purpose, method of use, complementarity, competition, channels, public); Nice class numbers do not decide similarity (Art. 33(7)). Absolute grounds: PRIME is laudatory and SCORE denotes a rating, so the compound is at its weakest for services whose subject is a score (credit, risk or financial-health scores) and stronger for research, monitoring and event intelligence; the components being ordinary words does not by itself make the whole descriptive (Biomild test). Consequences if a claimant succeeded in the EU: injunction and rebrand (Directive 2004/48 Art. 11), damages appropriate to the prejudice or a lump sum (Art. 13), costs (Art. 14), publication (Art. 15), plus opposition or invalidity against the founder's own filing; a UDRP decision can only transfer or cancel the domain.

## Appendix D. Domain-dispute analysis for primescore.ai

- The .ai ccTLD is subject to the UDRP administered by WIPO (WIPO .ai page). WIPO decided .ai cases under the UDRP both before and after the January 2025 registry migration to Identity Digital's back end (datamotion.ai DAI2024-0035; glide.ai DAI2026-0029; semify.ai DAI2026-0053). The registry's own FAQ answer on UDRP could not be read (JavaScript-rendered).
- Element (i), rights and confusing similarity: primescore.ai is identical to PRIMESCORE, but a complainant must hold trade-mark rights. The Indian applicant has an objected device-mark application and unregistered Indian use, so it must show acquired distinctiveness (Overview 1.3); a pending application alone is not enough (1.1.4). PrimeCounsel's application is dead. The US credit-repair businesses hold at most local unregistered rights in "Prime Score". People's Bank's loan product is a product name with no registration found.
- Element (ii), rights or legitimate interests: rests on demonstrable preparations (Overview 2.2): the public repository, the live landing page, dated engine commits, ADRs and design records. Preserve them and never rewrite history.
- Element (iii), bad faith at registration and in use: the domain was registered on 2026-09-21, after the Indian company's rights arose, so the timing rule in Overview 3.8.1 gives no protection. The defence is absence of targeting and an independent reason for the name; recent panels refused to infer bad faith where the complainant's reputation at the acquisition date was not shown (glide.ai; semify.ai). Never offer the domain for sale to a namesake; keep the site substantive (passive holding, Overview 3.3); avoid credit-score positioning.
- Remedies and territory: a UDRP decision transfers or cancels the domain; damages, injunctions against business use and costs come only from a court in a territory where the claimant holds rights and the founder trades. An Indian registration gives no rights in Romania; a Romanian or EU registration by the founder gives none in India.

## Appendix E. Adversarial reconciliation

Best case for a claimant. (1) PRIMESCORE FINTECH: it used PRIMESCORE in trade from 2025 and filed in India in 2024; the founder adopted the identical word in September 2026 for a portfolio that includes a personal-finance product; under Romanian Law 84/1998 Art. 6(3)(f) a mark protected abroad plus bad faith blocks registration, and Paris Art. 8 protects trade names without registration; a consumer-finance product under PRIMESCORE would be a related service. (2) Owners of SCORE composites in classes 9, 36 and 42 (FINSCORE and others) could oppose a class 36 or class 9 filing on Art. 8(1)(b), arguing the same structure and similar goods. (3) Amazon could argue Art. 8(5) against a PRIME-formative in classes 9, 35 and 42. (4) In the US, credit-repair users of "Prime Score" could assert §1125(a) rights against a consumer-finance launch in their localities. (5) On the domain, the Indian party can point to the founder's awareness of primescore.in, which appears in the founder's own brief.

Best case for the founder. (1) No PRIMESCORE mark exists in EU, Romanian, WIPO or UK data; the Indian device application is not registered and has been objected; Indian trade-name use gives no EU rights, and the bad-faith route requires dishonest intention, not knowledge (CJEU Lindt C-529/07; Koton C-104/18 P), which is implausible for a Romanian market-intelligence product that does not compete with an Indian credit-repair consultancy. (2) SCORE is a weak, widely shared element; many two-element SCORE marks coexist, so the prefix dominates the comparison and PRIME differs from FIN, C, GO, OK and ID. (3) PRIME is laudatory and common; Amazon's PRIME is not a single word with SCORE. (4) The US users are local and descriptive; the founder's professional products are not credit counselling. (5) The founder has contemporaneous evidence of independent development and a public site, and neither potential complainant has shown reputation at the acquisition date.

Assumptions challenged. "No TMview record means clear": TMview can lag the offices by days or weeks, its contains-search is not a similarity or phonetic search, and direct EUIPO, OSIM and WIPO searches were blocked; the negative is credible for a preliminary check only. "The Indian company has no EU rights": true for Art. 8(2) rights, but the bad-faith and trade-name routes exist and depend on the founder's intent and the date he learned of primescore.in, which this investigation could not establish. "PrimeScore is descriptive": that weakens both the founder's own protection and third-party claims against him; it argues for narrow specifications rather than for abandoning the name. "The domain has no history": only the current registration object is visible; a prior expired registration was not checked. A "by PrimeScore" endorsement is still use of the mark, so it does not insulate the finance product legally; it only changes the commercial impression, which is the reason for the carve-out.

Four paths compared. Keep PrimeScore across all five products: cheapest, but puts the consumer-finance product under the most exposed and least distinctive use of the mark. Limit its scope to the professional products: keeps one identity, avoids the class 36 exposure and the credit-score reading where it matters; recommended. Independent brands with or without endorsement: five clearances and five domains for one person; not sustainable. Replace before launch: cheap today, but every real-word replacement screened is no cleaner than PrimeScore and none has an available domain; justified only if the professional opinion on PRIMESCORE is negative.
